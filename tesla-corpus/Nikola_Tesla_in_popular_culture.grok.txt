Literature and science fiction have long mythologized Tesla, particularly in early 20th-century works that drew directly from his real-life claims of communicating with Mars; J. Weldon Cobb's To Mars with Tesla (1901), serialized in magazines, features him inventing a signaling device for interplanetary contact, portraying him as a bridge between Earth and the cosmos . [2] H.G. Wells incorporated Tesla's Mars signaling into The First Men in the Moon (1901), using it as a plot device to reveal lunar transmissions, which helped cement his image as a prophetic inventor in speculative genres. [2] More recent portrayals extend to comics and graphic novels, where he often appears as a steampunk or superhero archetype, though his cultural resonance also manifests in humorous formats like the YouTube series Epic Rap Battles of History (2013), which dramatizes his "War of the Currents" with Edison through a viral rap confrontation that has garnered millions of views. [1] 
Beyond visual and literary media, Tesla's influence permeates music and digital culture; the American rock band Tesla, formed in 1981, drew its name from the inventor and sold over 14 million albums worldwide, with titles like The Great Radio Controversy (1989) nodding to his radio pioneering. [1] Online tributes, such as Google's 2009 Doodle featuring a Tesla coil animation for his birthday, and appearances in animated shows like Family Guy (2011), further illustrate his status as a symbol of innovative underdogs in geek and DIY communities. [1] [7] These representations collectively revive Tesla's legacy, transforming historical facts into narratives that celebrate his forward-thinking ethos amid ongoing fascination with his unrealized projects, such as the " death ray " weapon concept from the 1930s , as seen in recent works like the 2024 documentary Nikola Tesla : Waves of the Future . Books 
Fictional Appearances 
Nikola Tesla has appeared as a fictional character in numerous novels, often portrayed as an eccentric genius whose inventions blur the lines between science and the supernatural . Authors frequently draw on his real-life rivalry with Thomas Edison , his visionary ideas about wireless energy, and his reclusive later years to explore themes of innovation , isolation , and the human cost of brilliance. These depictions range from historical fiction to speculative narratives, emphasizing Tesla's role as a misunderstood pioneer. 
One of the earliest fictional portrayals is J. Weldon Cobb's 1901 serialized novel To Mars with Tesla , which features Tesla inventing a device for interplanetary communication, depicting him as a pioneer bridging Earth and other worlds through his signaling technology. [2] 
In Christopher Priest's 1995 novel The Prestige , Tesla serves as a pivotal supporting character, depicted as a reclusive inventor in Colorado Springs who constructs a mysterious electromagnetic machine for the protagonist, a stage magician obsessed with perfecting an illusion. The device, inspired by Tesla's real experiments with alternating current and wireless transmission, enables teleportation-like effects, heightening the story's themes of deception and duality. [9] [10] 
Thomas Pynchon's sprawling 2006 epic Against the Day features Tesla in a cameo role amid its panoramic depiction of early 20th-century America and Europe . Set partly in Colorado Springs, the novel portrays Tesla conducting grandiose experiments with a massive electrical tower aimed at harnessing atmospheric energy, symbolizing the era's technological optimism and geopolitical tensions. His presence underscores the book's exploration of anarchism , science , and conspiracy . [11] [12] 
Samantha Hunt's 2008 novel The Invention of Everything Else centers on Tesla during his final days in 1943 at the Hotel New Yorker, where he resides in isolation, pilfering electricity and communing with pigeons. The story weaves a surreal friendship between the aging inventor and a young chambermaid, Louisa, as Tesla reflects on his life's inventions and unfulfilled dreams, blending historical detail with magical realism. The book was a finalist for the Orange Prize for Fiction. [13] [14] 
The young adult Accelerati Trilogy by Neal Shusterman (2013–2016), starting with Tesla's Attic , involves teenagers discovering and activating Tesla's hidden inventions in an attic, leading to adventures blending science fiction and humor, portraying Tesla as a posthumous influencer of modern chaos through his legacy of gadgets. 
Graham Moore's 2016 historical thriller The Last Days of Night dramatizes the " War of the Currents " between Edison and George Westinghouse , with Tesla as a key ally to Westinghouse, portrayed as an enigmatic Serbian inventor whose alternating current system promises to revolutionize power distribution. Through the eyes of a young lawyer, the novel highlights Tesla's intellectual clashes and contributions to the battle over electrification, culminating in the adoption of AC power at Niagara Falls . [15] [16] 
In Arthur Shattuck O'Keefe's occult horror novel The Spirit Phone (2022), set in 1899, Tesla joins forces with English occultist Aleister Crowley to confront the enigma of Thomas Edison's newly-invented "spirit phone," a device to speak with the dead. The somewhat germophobic and straitlaced Tesla and the hedonistic, cocky young Crowley act as foils to one another. Tesla's rivalry with Edison is rendered in a nuanced manner, with no depiction of absolute blame upon either Edison or Tesla. [17] [18] 
Other works include Anthony Flacco's 2013 novel In the Matter of Nikola Tesla: A Romance of the Mind , which fictionalizes Tesla's life as an espionage-tinged tale of genius and intrigue, and Rob Dircks's 2015 comedic adventure Where the Hell is Tesla? , where the inventor is revived in a modern-day quest involving interdimensional travel and his death ray prototype. More recently, Sharon Rich and D.D. Vujic's 2024 biographical novel Tesla: Manhattan Dove to Queens Seagull explores Tesla's later life, blending fact and fiction to reveal aspects of his personal relationships and unrealized visions. [19] [20] [21] These portrayals often amplify Tesla's mythical status in popular imagination, focusing on his overlooked legacy. 
Allusions 
Nikola Tesla's inventions and visionary concepts have frequently served as subtle inspirations in literature, particularly in science fiction, where his ideas on wireless energy and communication underpin thematic elements without him appearing as a character. For instance, in H.G. Wells ' 1901 novel The First Men in the Moon , the plot involves protagonist Bedford receiving and decoding radio signals from the Moon via inventor Cavor's device, directly alluding to Tesla's 1899 claims of detecting extraterrestrial signals from Mars using his Colorado Springs apparatus. This interplanetary communication motif reflects Tesla's pioneering work in radio transmission, which Wells adapted to explore themes of scientific hubris and cosmic contact. [2] 
Early 20th-century science fiction editor and author Hugo Gernsback extensively incorporated Tesla's innovations into his speculative narratives, embedding allusions to technologies like wireless power and high-frequency electricity . In Gernsback's 1911 serialized novel Ralph 124C 41+ , the protagonist employs advanced devices for remote energy transmission and telephotography, concepts drawn from Tesla's experiments with alternating current and the Wardenclyffe Tower project, portraying a utopian future powered by such "scientifiction" ideas. Gernsback's magazines, including Modern Electrics and Electrical Experimenter , further propagated these allusions by publishing stories seeded with Tesla's principles, such as electromagnetic pulses in "The Magnetic Storm" (1918), influencing the genre's depiction of futuristic electricity . [22] [23] 
In modern literature, Tesla's legacy appears through indirect references to his inventions as symbols of ingenuity. Lemony Snicket's A Series of Unfortunate Events series (1999–2006) features multiple nods to Tesla: protagonist Violet Baudelaire's name alludes to the early 20th-century violet ray device, a quack medical tool based on Tesla's high-voltage coil technology, while in The Austere Academy (2000), Violet explicitly wonders "what Nikola Tesla would do" to devise an escape from peril, invoking him as an archetypal inventor. These allusions highlight Tesla's enduring role as a cultural emblem of creative problem-solving amid adversity. [24] [25] 
Comics and Graphic Novels 
Fictional Appearances 
Nikola Tesla has appeared as a fictional character in various comics and graphic novels, often depicted as an eccentric genius inventor whose technologies combat villains or explore alternate histories. These portrayals frequently highlight his rivalry with Thomas Edison, wireless energy concepts, and mythical elements like death rays, blending historical facts with speculative adventure. 
In the 2006 Image Comics miniseries Five Fists of Science by Matt Fraction and Steven Sanders, Tesla teams up with Mark Twain and others to build giant robots and advanced weapons aimed at ending war through superior technology. The story fictionalizes a business venture to sell peace-enforcing inventions to world powers, pitting them against Edison and occult threats in a steampunk narrative. [26] 
The 2017-2018 Titan Comics series Minky Woodcock: The Girl Who Electrified Tesla by Cynthia von Buhler features Tesla in his later years as a reclusive figure in New York, hired by detective Minky Woodcock to validate his " death ray " invention amid Nazi espionage plots funded by J.P. Morgan Jr . The pulp-style story incorporates real historical details of Tesla's final days with erotic and mystery elements. [27] 
In the 2023 Titan Comics graphic novel The Three Ghosts of Tesla by Richard Marazano and Guilhem (English edition), Tesla survives into World War II in an alternate history, using supernatural abilities and inventions to battle German forces from a Manhattan hotel. The narrative weaves his eccentricities with ghostly elements, portraying him as humanity's last hope against invasion. [28] 
Tesla also appears in Marvel Comics, notably in the 2010-2012 S.H.I.E.L.D. series by Jonathan Hickman , as a member of the immortal "Brotherhood of S.H.I.E.L.D." alongside Leonardo da Vinci and Isaac Newton , defending Earth from multidimensional threats using his electrical expertise. He is depicted as a long-lived inventor who faked his death in 1943 and later operates as the armored "Night Machine." [29] 
Other examples include the 2008 educational comic Nikola Tesla and the Electric Fair by the American Physical Society , which dramatizes his rivalry with Edison at the 1893 Chicago World's Fair, and the ongoing Atomic Robo series (2007-present, Red 5 Comics) by Brian Clevinger and Scott Wegener, where Tesla creates the sentient robot protagonist in 1923, appearing in flashbacks as a mentor figure battling global dangers. [30] [31] 
Allusions 
Nikola Tesla's inventions and persona have inspired subtle references in comics and graphic novels, particularly in science fiction and superhero genres, where his wireless power and high-voltage concepts influence technologies and character archetypes without direct appearances. 
In the 2008 comic Nikola Tesla and the Electric Fair , Tesla's alternating current system powers the Chicago World's Fair in a fictionalized rivalry with Edison, alluding to his real 1893 demonstrations that symbolized the "electrical age," educating readers on electromagnetism through narrative devices like glowing exhibits. [32] 
The Atomic Robo series (Red 5 Comics, 2007-present) alludes to Tesla's legacy through the robot hero's nuclear-powered design and action-scientist team, drawing from his experiments with high-frequency electricity and robotics visions, portraying a world where his unfulfilled ideas enable pulp adventures against mad scientists and monsters. [31] 
In superhero comics, Tesla's work subtly informs characters like Marvel's Electro (Max Dillon), whose electricity manipulation and origin involving power plant accidents echo Tesla coil experiments, symbolizing the dangers and wonders of high-voltage innovation in stories exploring human augmentation. [33] These allusions reinforce Tesla's role as a foundational figure in depictions of futuristic energy and genius in graphic storytelling. 
Film 
Fictional Appearances 
Nikola Tesla has appeared as a fictional character in numerous films, often portrayed as an eccentric genius whose inventions blur the lines between science and the supernatural . Filmmakers frequently draw on his real-life rivalry with Thomas Edison , his visionary ideas about wireless energy, and his reclusive later years to explore themes of innovation , isolation, and the human cost of brilliance. These depictions range from biographical dramas to speculative narratives, emphasizing Tesla's role as a misunderstood pioneer. 
In Christopher Nolan's 2006 film The Prestige , David Bowie portrays Tesla as a reclusive inventor in Colorado Springs who constructs a mysterious electromagnetic machine for the protagonist, a stage magician obsessed with perfecting an illusion. The device, inspired by Tesla's real experiments with alternating current and wireless transmission, enables cloning effects, heightening the story's themes of deception and duality. [34] 
Benedikt Erlingsson's 2017 drama The Current War (released in 2019) features Nicholas Hoult as Tesla, depicting his alliance with George Westinghouse in the " War of the Currents " against Edison. The film highlights Tesla's development of alternating current and its adoption for powering Niagara Falls , portraying him as an enigmatic figure driving technological revolution. [35] 
Michael Almereyda's 2020 biographical drama Tesla stars Ethan Hawke as the inventor, exploring his personal struggles, innovations in AC power and wireless technology, and rivalry with Edison. The film blends historical facts with stylized elements, including fourth-wall breaks, to underscore Tesla's underappreciated contributions to the electrical age. [36] 
Earlier portrayals include Krsto Papić's 1980 Yugoslavian film The Secret of Nikola Tesla , where Petar Božović plays Tesla, focusing on his life from education to the Wardenclyffe Tower project, with Orson Welles as J.P. Morgan . The narrative emphasizes conspiracy elements around his inventions and financial backers. [37] 
Allusions 
Nikola Tesla's inventions and visionary concepts have frequently served as subtle inspirations in films, particularly in science fiction, where his ideas on wireless energy and communication underpin thematic elements without him appearing as a character. For instance, in Brad Bird's 2015 Disney film Tomorrowland , Tesla is referenced as a co-founder (with Thomas Edison , Gustave Eiffel , and Jules Verne ) of the secret inventor society " Plus Ultra ," which accesses a parallel utopian dimension. This alludes to his forward-thinking ethos and collaborations, tying into the plot's exploration of innovation and futurism . [38] 
Jim Jarmusch's 2013 vampire drama Only Lovers Left Alive includes multiple allusions to Tesla through the character Adam ( Tom Hiddleston ), a reclusive musician whose inventions and car are powered by Tesla's wireless energy transmission concepts. Tesla is mentioned in dialogues and featured on Adam's "wall of heroes," symbolizing enduring genius amid themes of decay and creativity. [39] 
In Jennifer Baer's 2016 mystery thriller The American Side , Tesla's missing plans for a " death ray " weapon and wireless energy experiments drive the plot, alluding to his unrealized projects and their potential geopolitical impact without depicting him directly. The story uses his legacy to frame a noir investigation at Niagara Falls . [40] 
Television 
Fictional Appearances 
Nikola Tesla has been portrayed as a fictional character in various television series and episodes, often depicted as an eccentric inventor whose real-life innovations are dramatized or exaggerated for narrative purposes. These portrayals typically highlight his rivalry with Thomas Edison , his groundbreaking work on alternating current , and his visionary ideas about wireless energy and advanced technology , blending historical facts with speculative elements to explore themes of genius , isolation, and scientific progress. 
In the Doctor Who episode "Nikola Tesla's Night of Terror" (2020), Croatian actor Goran Višnjić plays Tesla as a determined inventor in 1903 New York, defending his laboratory and the world from extraterrestrial invaders known as the Flux using his electrical experiments and Wardenclyffe Tower prototype. The episode fictionalizes Tesla's struggles with funding and recognition while incorporating his real historical context. [41] 
The Canadian science fiction series Sanctuary (2008–2011) features Jonathon Young as Nikola Tesla in a recurring role across multiple seasons. Portrayed as an immortal "abnormal" human enhanced with vampire-like abilities and electromagnetic powers derived from his experiments, Tesla is a brilliant but arrogant ally to protagonist Helen Magnus . The character draws on Tesla's historical persona, including his work on high-voltage electricity, while placing him in a modern supernatural world. [42]
